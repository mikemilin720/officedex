# OfficeDex PPT Launch Video Design

Date: 2026-07-13

Status: Approved creative design

Primary language: English

## 1. Objective

Create a polished 75–90 second product launch video that explains the redesigned OfficeDex PPT workflow to prospective overseas users and motivates them to download OfficeDex immediately.

The film must communicate one central idea:

> OfficeDex no longer treats presentation generation as a one-shot output. It provides a visible, controllable journey from idea and story structure to slide generation, preview, and AI-assisted editing.

The film is a product journey, not a tutorial and not a comparison against the previous version. A viewer who has never used OfficeDex should understand the value without knowing the product history.

## 2. Audience, Channels, and CTA

- Primary audience: prospective overseas users who create presentations for work.
- Primary channels: OfficeDex website and X.
- Secondary channels: other social platforms using cutdowns from the same master footage.
- Primary call to action: **Download OfficeDex**.
- On-camera talent: none.
- Presentation: real product interface, English narration, music, sound design, and burned-in English captions.
- Silent-playback requirement: the complete story must remain understandable with audio muted.

## 3. Creative Direction

### 3.1 Story approach

Use the approved **Product Journey** structure. Follow one realistic prompt from initial request to a reviewed and edited deck.

Demo topic:

> Launch strategy for a new AI productivity app

On-screen demo prompt:

> Create a launch strategy presentation for a new AI productivity app. Define the target audience, positioning, launch channels, a 90-day rollout plan, and success metrics. Make it clear, visual, and suitable for an executive review.

The featured follow-up edit is:

> Make this launch timeline more visual.

The edit should target slide 6 in the prepared demo deck unless the final generated deck places the launch timeline on a different slide. In that case, use the actual timeline slide and keep the narration generic enough not to mention a page number.

### 3.2 Narrative arc and timing

| Time | Story beat | Product evidence | Narration purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–6s | Hook | Warm-paper title frame leading into OfficeDex | Establish that presentation generation should not be a one-shot gamble. |
| 6–15s | The request | New PPTX task, prompt entry, Generate action | Show that users describe the communication goal rather than manually specifying every slide. |
| 15–35s | Visible planning | Idea, Story Beats, Chapters, and per-page Outlines | Demonstrate that the story can be inspected, refined, and approved in stages. |
| 35–51s | Deck construction | Page generation, assembly progress, and layout-quality checks | Show OfficeDex creating the real presentation rather than stopping at an outline. |
| 51–64s | Real preview | PPTist editor, slide rail, cover, strategy, channel, and timeline slides | Prove that the generated deck can be reviewed inside the workflow. |
| 64–78s | AI editing | Select the timeline slide or an element, send the edit request, review the proposed change, and apply it | Show that generation is the beginning of an editable workflow, not the end. |
| 78–85s | Payoff and CTA | Improved slide, deck overview, OfficeDex brand, download frame | Convert the product demonstration into a clear download action. |

Target master duration: approximately 85 seconds.

## 4. Visual System

Use the approved **Paper & Ink** direction. Motion may be cinematic, but all styling must remain consistent with the current OfficeDex interface.

### 4.1 Palette

| Role | Color | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm paper | `#FCFAF2` | Primary film background and editorial title cards |
| Soft surface | `#F0EEE6` | Secondary panels and transitions |
| Canvas | `#FFFFFF` | Real UI windows and clean content areas |
| Primary ink | `#05101A` | Main buttons, strongest titles, final payoff frame |
| Body ink | `#1A2530` | Body copy and captions |
| Hairline | `#E6E4D8` | Borders and separators |
| Teal accent | `#006876` | Interaction guidance and restrained highlights |
| Logo purple | Current OfficeDex logo purple | Brand identity moments only; do not recolor the full interface |
| Success green | `#1AAE39` | Connection, completion, or success states only |

Do not introduce a generic neon-blue technology palette. Do not recolor the captured product UI.

### 4.2 Typography and motion

- Headlines: Plus Jakarta Sans where available; use Inter as the fallback.
- Captions and supporting text: Inter.
- Captions: maximum two lines, high contrast, positioned within safe margins for website and X playback.
- Motion ratio: approximately 80% controlled product focus and 20% bold editorial title moments.
- Preferred transitions: paper panels sliding, cropping, unfolding, or revealing the next UI region.
- Avoid: gaming-style light streaks, excessive glow, constant zooming, glitch effects, and decorative motion that competes with the interface.
- Use soft editorial shadows matching the product's card and window depth.

### 4.3 Product capture treatment

- The real OfficeDex UI is the hero.
- Use crop, focus, cursor movement, and gentle camera animation to guide attention.
- Preserve the interface's actual warm whites, ink-black controls, purple logo, and semantic status colors.
- Use the ink-black film background only for the opening emphasis and final CTA, not as a replacement for the current product palette.

## 5. Audio Design

- Voice: warm, confident female voice.
- Accent: natural international English or light American English.
- Delivery: calm and assured, not exaggerated advertising speech.
- Target pace: approximately 145–155 words per minute, with pauses for product actions.
- Music: restrained modern electronic or ambient track with a steady build and a clean payoff near the final CTA.
- UI sound effects: limited to a few meaningful clicks, stage transitions, an apply action, and a completion tone.
- Mix rule: narration is always clearly louder than music; music should support rhythm without masking product sounds or consonants.

### 5.1 Working narration draft

> A great presentation shouldn’t be a one-shot gamble. Tell OfficeDex what you want to communicate—not how to build every slide. It turns your idea into a story you can see, refine, and approve. Review the story beats. Shape the chapters. Check every slide outline before the deck is built. Then OfficeDex creates the real presentation, page by page, with visuals, structure, and layout checks along the way. Preview every slide without leaving your workflow. And when the first version is ready, keep creating. Select a slide—or even an element—and describe what should change. Review the proposed edit, apply it, and continue until the presentation feels right. From first thought to final polish, OfficeDex gives you a presentation workflow you can actually control. Download OfficeDex and create your next deck.

This draft must be timed against the approved picture edit. Shorten pauses or individual sentences before increasing the speaking rate.

## 6. Production Workflow

Use a free-first workflow suitable for a first-time video maker.

1. Prepare and verify one complete OfficeDex demo run.
2. Record 8–12 short screen clips instead of one continuous performance.
3. Build the picture-first rough cut in CapCut Desktop Free.
4. Add Paper & Ink titles, focus masks, captions, and restrained camera motion.
5. Generate the English female narration using CapCut text-to-speech or another free quota.
6. Add free commercially usable music from a source such as YouTube Audio Library or Pixabay Music, verifying the license at download time.
7. Mix, caption, export, and review the master.
8. Create platform cutdowns only after the master is approved.

Fallback editor: DaVinci Resolve Free. It is more capable but has a steeper learning curve, so it is not the default for the first version.

Temporary timing voice: macOS system speech may be used during rough editing, but it must be replaced before release.

## 8. Recording Preparation

- Use an English-only demo workspace and clean chat history.
- Use the current release candidate intended for the launch demonstration.
- Prepare and verify the successful final deck before recording the first clip.
- Hide API keys, account details, local file paths, notifications, private chat names, and unrelated projects.
- Use a neutral test account and non-sensitive demo data.
- Fix display scaling, app window size, and recording resolution for the entire project.
- Record at 1920×1080 or higher and 30 fps. Prefer a higher-resolution source if the machine performs smoothly, while keeping the final output at 1080p.
- Move the cursor deliberately. Pause briefly before each click and after each visible state change.
- Do not record narration at the same time as the screen.

## 10. Failure Handling

- If live generation is slow, use honest editorial time compression. Do not imply that a long operation completed instantly.
- If a live generation attempt fails, stop recording and return to the prepared verified task. Capture each required state independently.
- If the generated slide order differs from the design, target the actual timeline slide and avoid narration that states a fixed slide number.
- If AI editing produces an unsuitable result, refine the prompt and prepare a verified edit before recording. Do not fabricate a result outside the product.
- If CapCut or its voice service is unavailable, use DaVinci Resolve Free and a temporary system voice until a releasable voice can be generated.
- If a music source requires attribution, either include the required attribution in the publishing copy or select a different track with compatible terms.

## 11. Release QA

The video is ready only when all checks pass:

- Every claimed capability is visibly demonstrated in the current OfficeDex build.
- The multi-stage workflow is understandable without prior knowledge of the old version.
- The product UI keeps its current Paper & Ink colors and is never obscured by decorative overlays.
- No private data, local secrets, unrelated chats, notifications, or identifiable account details are visible.
- Captions are accurate, synchronized, and readable on a phone at normal X playback size.
- The entire story remains understandable when autoplay is muted.
- Narration is intelligible and consistently louder than music.
- There is no audio clipping, abrupt cut, dropped frame, frozen cursor, or accidental hover state.
- The OfficeDex name and Download OfficeDex CTA remain readable for at least 2.5 seconds.
- The final exported file is tested in a desktop browser, on a phone, on the target website placement, and in an unpublished X draft before release.

## 12. Scope Boundary

This design covers the launch film and its derivative cutdowns. It does not include product UI changes, a new website landing page, paid media buying, a full tutorial series, or localization beyond retaining a clean master for later use.
